1. Core Idea: SPMSM vs IPMSM

Surface PMSM (SPMSM): Magnets are mounted on the surface of the rotor. The magnetic gap is uniform, leading to equal inductances (\(L_d \approx L_q\)).

Interior PMSM (IPMSM): Magnets are buried inside the rotor. The iron paths vary, creating magnetic saliency (\(L_q > L_d\)), which produces additional reluctance torque.

2. Mathematical Modeling of PMSM

Voltage Model (d-q frame)

The \(d\) and \(q\) axes are cross-coupled by the motor speed \(\omega_e\). Feedforward decoupling is required for high-performance control.

$$ v_d = R_s i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q $$ $$ v_q = R_s i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \phi) $$

Electromagnetic Torque

$$ T_e = \frac{3}{2} \frac{P}{2} \left[ \phi i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q \right] $$
  • Magnetic Torque: \(\propto \phi i_q\)
  • Reluctance Torque: \(\propto (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\)

Mechanical Dynamics

The motor acts as a transducer. We must produce enough Electromagnetic Torque (\(T_e\)) to overcome inertia (\(J\)), friction (\(B\)), and load (\(T_L\)).

$$ T_e = J \frac{d\omega_m}{dt} + B\omega_m + T_L $$

3. Reference Frame Transformations

1. Electrical vs Mechanical Speed

$$ \omega_e = \frac{P}{2} \omega_m $$

2. Clarke Transform (abc → αβ)

Maps 3-phase components to a 2D stationary orthogonal frame.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} i_a \\ i_b \\ i_c \end{bmatrix} $$

3. Park Transform (αβ → dq)

Maps stationary 2D to the spinning rotor reference frame.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix} $$

4. Inverse Park (dq → αβ)

Used to convert the voltage commands back to the stationary frame for the inverter.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha \\ v_\beta \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & -\sin\theta_e \\ \sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_d \\ v_q \end{bmatrix} $$

4. Modulation Techniques (SPWM vs SVPWM)

SPWM Phase References

Sinusoidal PWM directly uses pure sine waves. It suffers from poor DC bus utilization.

$$ v_a^* = V_m \sin(\omega t) $$ $$ v_b^* = V_m \sin\left(\omega t - \frac{2\pi}{3}\right) $$ $$ v_c^* = V_m \sin\left(\omega t + \frac{2\pi}{3}\right) $$ $$ m_a = \frac{V_m}{V_{dc} / 2} $$

Space Vector PWM (SVPWM)

SVPWM implicitly injects a zero-sequence (3rd harmonic) signal into the reference waveforms, creating a "saddle" shape. This prevents clipping at the peaks, unlocking ~15% more voltage (\(m_a \approx 1.15\)).
